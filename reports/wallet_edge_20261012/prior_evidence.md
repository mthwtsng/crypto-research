## Existing wallet scripts and why their output is not reusable evidence

The scratch scripts are useful as historical leads, but their methods do not establish the addressed wallets' entry rules:

- `study_tracked_bots.py` fetches current SOL balances for 22 accounts and prints them. It does not reconstruct closed trades, token inventory, cost basis, fees, or realized PnL.
- `deep_bot_analysis.py` looks at at most 15 recent signatures per selected wallet and samples up to eight transactions. It estimates wallet SOL balance change and token-balance deltas, then guesses venue from program IDs. `pfeeUx...` is the Pump fees program; the Pump AMM program is `pAMMBay...`, so its PumpSwap label can be false.
- `study_top_bots.py` samples recent signatures and the first few successful transactions. Its displayed SOL totals are hard-coded labels, not computed performance.
- `study_entry_triggers.py` only runs a hard-coded Botty example. It asks for the latest 100 mint signatures and treats the oldest returned signature as creation, which is not a genesis check. It does not cover every address or trade.
- `inspect_bottom_bidder.py`, `inspect_pre_buy.py`, and `inspect_snippebot_buy.py` inspect one or a few hand-picked transactions and short recent signature windows, not a complete creation-to-exit history.

Across these scripts, the event parser accepts arbitrary `Program data` records at hard-coded byte offsets without checking the event discriminator, the emitting program's successful invocation ancestry, or full transaction success. Several paths treat account-key zero as the trading wallet, which can be a fee payer or sponsor; balance deltas and a payer identity alone do not establish the trader. `maxSupportedTransactionVersion: 0` also excludes newer versioned transactions. The scripts print terminal output rather than saving a reproducible trade ledger. Their results should not be used to infer reserves at entry, wallet profitability, copy delay, or a copy-trading edge.

## Reusable authenticated decoding and history work

The strongest existing Pump event decoder is [`research/day_general_20261004/helpers/decoder.py`](../../research/day_general_20261004/helpers/decoder.py). It reads the saved official Pump IDL, checks event discriminators, tracks nested program invocation success, rejects failed transactions, and emits typed Trade/Create events. The runtime counterpart is [`src/bot/pump_receipt_decoder.rs`](../../src/bot/pump_receipt_decoder.rs), which also retains fee payer/signers, transaction index, token-balance coverage, and mint-scoped custody coverage. [`helpers/receipt_custody.py`](../../research/day_general_20261004/helpers/receipt_custody.py) is the related owner/token-account custody helper. For any later replay, the approved engine family is `research/day_general_20261004/helpers/{build.py,replay.py}` plus `base_engine.py` and `clock.py`; the scratch wallet scripts are not a replay engine.

The clearest complete-history recipe is [`research/wallet_curve_entry_discovery_20261007/recover_trajectories.py`](../../research/wallet_curve_entry_discovery_20261007/recover_trajectories.py): authenticate the wallet's successful Pump curve buy; bind its mint to one curve; collect successful curve-address transactions from creation through 600 seconds; require provider transaction indexes; sort by `(slot, transactionIndex)`; and freeze 10 fixed-hash entries per wallet. Its selected wallets were `88887...`, `9999hu...`, `sssss...`, and `3VUN...`, chosen from a universe historically selected for profitability; selection was retrospective and no individual sampled trade was profit-filtered. This is not a sample of the seven addresses. The freeze, summaries, and research reports remain, but the `trajectories/` payload directory and the imported `automated_wallet_expansion_20261006/collect_wallets.py` dependency are absent from the current checkout, so the 40 full histories cannot be re-opened from these files alone.

## Comparison with the canonical strategies and current results

The current canonical controls in [`research/MASTER_STRATEGY_REGISTRY.md`](../../research/MASTER_STRATEGY_REGISTRY.md) are structurally different from copying the seven wallet addresses:

- S1 enters after observed creator selling/exhaustion, a 25–60% drawdown into a 1–5 SOL curve, and fresh outside-buy confirmation near the trough.
- S2 enters a 10.8–14 SOL shelf only after age, breadth, holder-share, recent buyer-only transactions, and a fresh 30-second high.
- S3 buys a mature 8–18 SOL sell shock only after two distinct buyers complete separate buys within two slots.
- S4 buys an all-time high in the 16–22 SOL band with broad historical buyers and a large outside buy in the signal transaction.
- S5 enters a 72 SOL crossing with holder breadth and positive outside flow, then carries custody through authenticated migration liquidity.

## Useful next evidence

For the seven-address question, start with each address's complete successful transaction history, classify owned token balance changes and fee payer/signers separately, then bind every Pump event to its discriminator, invocation and mint. Reconstruct each token's curve and actual post-migration pool tape from creation, with same-slot transaction indexes; identify buy/sell receipts and reserve state at the wallet's own execution. Compare entry snapshots against same-coin non-entry states and preserve unsuccessful/failed sends separately. Only after the empirical entry signatures are frozen should central-placement copy fills and the canonical S1–S5 controls be compared. That is the smallest path from the current sparse overlaps to an auditable answer; this inventory establishes no profitable wallet-copy strategy.
