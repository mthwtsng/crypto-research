## Receipt cohort and cross-case comparison

The frozen second tape contains 385 distinct signatures and 385 decoded SOL-quoted events in the observed interval 23:35:20–23:38:36 UTC. Excluding the two creator events leaves 383 user events from 234 external wallets: 288 buys and 95 sells. There are no signatures with multiple distinct user wallets and no signature containing both directions. Of the 234 external wallets, 36 have at least one buy and one sell. In slot/transaction-index order, those wallets have 49 adjacent direction changes; 8 changes across 8 wallets occur within five integer event-timestamp seconds, 13 within 10 seconds, and 27 within 30 seconds. The event timestamp is only second-granular. These are follow-up candidates, not proof of wash trading: a buy followed by a sell can also be ordinary trading, arbitrage, or market making, and none of the 385 individual receipts is itself a buy-and-sell round trip.

Using the frozen Cvy `target_wallets.json` address set (392 addresses) and the second tape, the exact external-user intersection is **4 addresses**: 4/234 (1.71%) of observed second-coin users and 4/392 (1.02%) of the Cvy target set. All four bought both mints. Three had both buy and sell events on Cvy; one was buy-only there. In the second tape, three are buy-only and one buys then sells. The second-tape buys of the first two overlapping addresses occurred at 23:36:23.996 and 23:36:24.711, before the paper entry; the other two overlap addresses first bought the second coin at 23:38:13.539 and 23:38:29.363, after it. Thus the final four-address intersection was not an entry-time four-wallet cohort.

For those four addresses, the gap from their last observed Cvy receipt to their first second-coin receipt was 13.69, 19.71, 14.69 and 22.84 minutes, respectively. The detector’s exact-address buyer history expires after 10 minutes. It therefore cannot carry these Cvy links into the second coin’s entry-time cross-launch evaluation. Its code also forms a cross-launch candidate only when at least three currently linked holder wallets overlap, and still passes `cross_launch_probe_complete=false` unconditionally. The snapshot’s zero cross-launch records are consistent with those limits; they do not show that no wallets ever appeared across launches. Sources: `src/bin/curve_detector.rs:297-335`, `387-410`, `536-570`; `src/bot/fake_coin_detector.rs:774-808`.

The fee-payer comparison has two different signals. Two payer addresses sponsor multiple observed users in both coin tapes: `AgmLJ...` paid for 42 Cvy target users and two second-coin users; `FHpcN...` paid for 15 Cvy target users and one second-coin user. The recipient sets of those two payer addresses do not intersect across the cases. Separately, the four exact wallet overlaps were also their own fee payer in both tapes. There is no same-signature multi-user transaction in either case. Shared fee payers merit ancestry and service-wallet checks, but a payer is not necessarily the controller or funder of the token buyer; these counts alone do not prove related ownership. The four exact overlaps are the strongest direct cross-case identity match, but are a small cohort rather than evidence that the full two buyer populations are the same group.

Repeated `transaction_fingerprint` values are also weak here. In the second tape, 288 external buys use 68 fingerprint values; 15 values are shared by at least two different buyers, and the most common appears for 134 buyers. The fingerprint deliberately hashes route/program structure while omitting dynamic account keys and most route data, so this high reuse is consistent with generic transaction structure. The detector itself labels fingerprints diagnostic-only and cannot use them as hard-block corroboration. ## What this supports and what it does not

The evidence supports three operational facts: the shadow ledger admitted a fresh Watch S5 paper entry; the entry screen had partial funding and cross-launch coverage and no qualifying detector cluster; and the two selected examples have four exact user-address overlaps plus two recurring fee-payer addresses whose recipient sets are disjoint. The additional selected funding sample exposes a two-wallet same-source pair holding slightly over 20%, but the source degree and full history remain unverified and the transfer is outside the launch window.

It does **not** establish that either mint’s rising volume was fabricated, that the four repeat buyers share an owner, that any payer controls its recipients, or that the second pool was rugged. Competing explanations include repeat retail/momentum traders, common trading automation or routing, third-party fee/funding services, and coordinated trading. The chosen sample is only two mints and the second receipt window ends before its full observed lifecycle, so it cannot answer whether the same cohort repeatedly targeted many launches.

Evidence-backed changes to consider:

1. Separate behavioral risk from coverage uncertainty in admission. If entry during partial-coverage Watch is unwanted, require a fresh clear screen for a configurable class of high-consequence entries or delay until a targeted recheck; count false skips from RPC outages and sample bounds. Keep Unknown fail-closed.
2. Persist exact-address cross-launch observations longer than 10 minutes and across restarts, with measured time/window coverage. A longer window could have surfaced these four addresses later, but only two were second-coin buyers at entry; the third and fourth arrived after entry, so do not claim the known cohort was block-eligible at that time.
3. Treat the two-wallet, 20.27% retention plus same-source prelaunch transfers as a separate concentration/funding review signal. A two-wallet alert can be more informative than dropping the minimum-wallet threshold for a hard block; the sample does not yet verify source degree or control.
4. Add event-level per-wallet inventory and buy/sell cadence features as review evidence, then require independent corroboration before blocking. Keep generic route fingerprints and shared payer addresses weak; control false positives from normal route reuse, arbitrage, market makers and copy trading.

No detector threshold or admission setting was changed. No profitability or PnL conclusion is made.
